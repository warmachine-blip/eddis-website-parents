## Understanding Radiofrequency Ablation

Before looking at other options, it helps to know what radiofrequency ablation is. This **minimally invasive procedure** uses an electrical current produced by a radio wave to heat up a small area of nerve tissue. The goal is to decrease pain signals from that specific area.

It is often discussed for chronic pain, especially in the [lower back](/back-pain) and [neck](/neck-pain). Many patients find it helps. Like all medical treatments, it does not guarantee a result.

### Reasons Radiofrequency Ablation Might Not Work

There could be various reasons why Radiofrequency Ablation doesn’t bring about the expected relief:

- **Misidentified Pain Source:** Sometimes, the exact source of pain might be misidentified, and the treated nerve might not be the primary pain generator.
- **Technical Factors:** Inaccuracies in needle placement or the area being too broad or narrow can influence the procedure’s effectiveness.
- **Individual Variability:** Everyone’s body reacts differently. Some might experience relief immediately, while others may find that the pain relief is only partial or temporary.

### What Next? Exploring Alternatives at HTx Pain Institute

Should you find yourself among those for whom **Radiofrequency Ablation** did not work as expected, our **Pain Management Clinic** offers other treatments. Your Pain Medicine Physician at **HTx Pain Institute** is dedicated to finding the right solution tailored to your needs.

- **Medications:** While medications are often used in tandem with other treatments, adjusting dosages or trying new medications can sometimes provide better pain relief.
- **Physical Therapy**: A regimen designed by a physical therapist can help alleviate pain by improving physical strength, endurance, and flexibility.
- **Epidural Steroid Injections:** Administered directly into the painful area of the spine, these injections can provide relief from inflammation.
- **Spinal Cord Stimulation**: A device is implanted beneath the skin to send a mild electric current to the spinal cord, helping mask pain signals.
- **Intracept:** For vertebral endplate pain, [Intracept](/intracept-procedure) targets the basivertebral nerve and leaves no implant behind.
- **Other Ablative Techniques:** There are other techniques, like chemical neurolysis, where chemicals are used to destroy the problematic nerve.
