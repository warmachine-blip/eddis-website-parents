## What is a Stellate Ganglion Block?

The stellate ganglion is a collection of nerves (sympathetic nerve ganglion) found in the neck. A stellate ganglion block (SGB) is an injection of a local anesthetic near these nerves. This block can help diagnose or treat pain originating from the sympathetic nervous system, which is often involved in various chronic pain conditions.

## The Procedure Explained

The SGB procedure is minimally invasive. The patient is positioned on their back, and after a local anesthetic is applied, the doctor uses fluoroscopy (a type of X-ray) to guide the needle to the stellate ganglion. The anesthetic is then injected, which temporarily blocks the sympathetic nerves.

## What it is used for

- **Nerve-related pain:** SGB is used for some nerve-related pain, including neuropathic pain, [complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS)](/other-pain-conditions), and some facial pain.
- **A diagnostic tool:** It can help look for the source of pain by temporarily blocking nerve signals.
- **Time:** The injection itself usually takes a few minutes. Plan on more time for check-in and a short observation period afterward.

## Who it may help

A stellate ganglion block may be discussed for people with:

- [Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS)](/other-pain-conditions).
- Herpes zoster pain (shingles).
- Phantom limb pain.
- Some headaches and facial pain.

## After the procedure

Some people notice a droopy eyelid, a hoarse voice, a stuffy nose, or warmth in the arm on the treated side. These effects usually fade within hours. Plan for someone else to drive you home that day. Patients are usually asked to rest on the day of the procedure.

## A reasonable next step

If you are living with one of these conditions, a pain medicine visit can tell you whether a stellate ganglion block is a reasonable next step.
